## Assessment

RD2's failure rate doubled to 3.4% of 7,454 jobs, but most of the rise came from real breaks. Of the 46 failures on `main`, 22 came from two breaks fixed during the week, the OBS trigger's rejected token (17 `Trigger OBS build` jobs, fixed by #846 on 09-24) and the stale generated TypeScript client (5 `Verify generated files` jobs, fixed by #838 on 09-23). Eight more were Dependabot runs, five of them the Kubernetes 1.37 bump that cannot resolve until #654 merges. On PR branches, 176 of the 210 failures are on seven branches that change the guest or bump a Go module the build cannot take yet, led by `move-guest-binary-sources` with 56. The flake to root-cause is the lost runner, which took down 10 BATS jobs (8 Windows, 2 macOS), 5 of them on `main`, in the same week RD1 lost 4 Windows e2e runners; the typeperf data that would show starvation never uploads from a dead runner, so that upload has to happen during the run. `bats-suite-timeout`, last week's top flake, went silent the week its cap rose from 60 to 75 minutes, so the silence does not yet show a faster suite.

<summary>How this report is built</summary>

`ci-sweep weekly --week 2026-W39` over `sweep.db`, one row per **failed job** — a run can fail on one matrix leg out of many, so the job is the unit.

- **Known flake** — a job whose log matched a `matchers.yaml` pattern. Pattern matching only, no LLM.
- **One flake per job** — when several matchers fire, the job counts under the one whose surface appears **earliest** in the log, because the first failure is the root and what follows is fallout: a suite killed at its wall-clock cap logs a failed wait on the way out. The others are recorded but not counted, and only a true tie is left uncounted.
- **Proven flake** — the identical job passed on a re-run of the same commit, so the code cannot be at fault.
- **Unrecognized** — no matcher fired. On `main` it earns analysis; on a PR branch it earns only a count, because PR failures are dev breakage, not CI health.
- **No log** — the log expired or was never uploaded, so the sweep could not classify the failure. A data gap, not a clean bill.
- **What failed** — for a known flake, the matcher's summary; otherwise the failing test and command, or the job error for a failure that never reached its tests. `--fetch` names the rest, at one download each.
- Counts cover only jobs the sweep recorded. A period never swept reads as zero failures.

</details>
