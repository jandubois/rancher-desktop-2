Assessment ¶

bats-suite-timeout is the whole story this week: 13 hits after eight weeks of zero, 11 of them on macos-15-intel bats-app. That leg is the only one anywhere near its limit, at 99% of the cap at p95, while every other suite sits at 52% or below. The headline counts fell against last week only because last week was exceptional: 90 unrecognized failures, most of them one branch's build breakage, plus one-week spikes of 18 go-proxy-module-fetch and 10 vault-login-transport-error.

The cause is PR #763, measured rather than inferred. It embeds the guest distro in the rdd binary and merged on 2026-09-15 at 22:48Z. The first breach on another branch came 17 minutes later, and the three before it were on the PR's own branch. Lima used to hand the image to the external multithreaded xz; rdd now decodes it in process on every VM create, and the median create went from 7s to 31s. bats-app creates a VM 16 times, so the decode alone adds 6.4 minutes. The suite's bats portion went from 41-43 minutes to 61-63 against a 60-minute cap.

The other 13 minutes have no explanation yet. Three tests roughly doubled: start VM with Kubernetes enabled 168s to 323s, KubernetesReady condition is True when k3s is running 174s to 312s, ImagePullRequest succeeds 125s to 230s. All three are guest-side k3s and image work, so the decode does not account for them. Run-to-run noise grew at the same time, from 4% between the two baseline jobs to 24% between the two post-merge ones. One hypothesis worth testing, unverified: the new decoder writes the image through pkg/sparse where xz -d wrote it whole, so the guest disk's layout may differ.

Only the Lima platforms pay it. Mean step minutes, before and after: macos-15-intel 41.8 to 54.5, ubuntu-latest 17.2 to 22.7, windows-latest 25.3 to 26.2. Windows runs its guest under WSL and has no image to decompress.

Nothing else moved. go-proxy-module-fetch fell from 18 to 6 and vault-login-transport-error from 10 to none, each after a one-week spike. The three TestStageGivesUpOnATricklingTransfer failures all predate their fix in PR #778, merged mid-week. The daily Dependabot failure on main is the standing k8s 1.36.4 to 1.37.0 break, not a flake, and the rddepman failures stopped when PR #753 merged on 2026-09-14.

What to fix, in order of impact ¶

Per-file cost was measured on job 105681124892, which ran all 206 tests in 3616s from the TAP plan to the last ok. Two files are 57% of the suite: kube-context at 1055.1s and app at 1008.0s.

1. Stop decompressing disks that nothing boots. 11% of the suite goes on exactly that. app.bats creates 11 instances and boots 3, spending 354.3s on decode; k3s-versions.bats creates one it never boots, for another 33.1s. The cause is that extractEmbeddedImage runs from limainstance.Create, gated only on the disk being absent, so creation pays the decode whatever spec.running says, and each delete_app wipes the instance dir so the next create starts over. Deferring the decode until a VM actually starts, or caching the bytes outside instance.Dir(), removes the whole class rather than trimming it. This is the fix the Makefile comment already asks for, and it helps ubuntu and local runs too.

2. Split bats-app in two. The only hard constraint is that app and kube-context go to different legs; no file shares state with another, so any partition is safe. Balanced on measured seconds: leg A takes app, demo, engine-containerd, image-docker and k3s-versions at 1794.4s, leg B takes engine-docker, kube-context, passthrough, set, start-stop and virtual-machine at 1821.8s. That is about 31 minutes each, or 39 minutes at the 24% noise seen this week, which is 65% of the cap. The cost is three more matrix legs across the three OSes, roughly 36 runner-minutes per CI run, since each leg pays its own rdd build. The balance is macOS-only: engine-containerd skips entirely on Windows.

Do not pick the files by grepping for running=true. That heuristic is wrong in both directions here, because setup_rdd_control_plane defaults to every controller: set and start-stop ask for "app" and create nothing despite their running=true lines, while k3s-versions asks for nothing, gets the lima controller, and creates an instance with no running=true anywhere in the file.

3. Explain the 13 minutes. Until someone does, nobody knows whether #763 has a second cost or whether something else regressed in the same window, and the cap is the only instrument currently detecting it.

4. Raise BATS_TIMEOUT only as a stopgap, and only alongside 3. A 75-minute cap clears every run measured since #763 merged: 13 of 51 macos steps ran past 60 minutes and none past 75. What it hides is growth between 63 and 75 minutes, which is most of a second regression the size of this one. Note what this week's main breach actually looked like: all 206 tests passed, and the wrapper killed the job 42.7s after the last ok, inside teardown_file. A bump alone would have turned it green without fixing anything. The cap has already gone 1800s to 2700s to 3600s this year; the last raise, on 2026-07-14, bought the eight quiet weeks this one ended.

How this report is built

ci-sweep weekly --week 2026-W38 over sweep.db, one row per failed job — a run can fail on one matrix leg out of many, so the job is the unit.

  • Known flake — a job whose log matched a matchers.yaml pattern. Pattern matching only, no LLM.
  • One flake per job — when several matchers fire, the job counts under the one whose surface appears earliest in the log, because the first failure is the root and what follows is fallout: a suite killed at its wall-clock cap logs a failed wait on the way out. The others are recorded but not counted, and only a true tie is left uncounted.
  • Proven flake — the identical job passed on a re-run of the same commit, so the code cannot be at fault.
  • Unrecognized — no matcher fired. On main it earns analysis; on a PR branch it earns only a count, because PR failures are dev breakage, not CI health.
  • No log — the log expired or was never uploaded, so the sweep could not classify the failure. A data gap, not a clean bill.
  • What failed — for a known flake, the matcher's summary; otherwise the failing test and command, or the job error for a failure that never reached its tests. --fetch names the rest, at one download each.
  • Counts cover only jobs the sweep recorded. A period never swept reads as zero failures.
