## Assessment

A quiet week by the numbers, with one exception that resolved itself mid-week.

Failures fell to 48 from the previous week's 142, well under the 66.0 four-week
average, and `main` held at 7. Almost all of that swing belongs to a single
branch. 36 of the week's 41 PR-branch failures are the etcd 3.7.0 dependabot
bump failing to compile, which measures one dependency bump. Strip it out and
the week is ordinary.

`bats-app` on macos-15-intel was killed 3 times, and every kill happened before
Tuesday 20:08 UTC, when PR #557 raised `BATS_TIMEOUT` from 45 to 60 minutes.
Since the raise, 9 macos `bats-app` runs have finished with zero kills, and one
of them ran 49m42s. Under the old 45-minute ceiling that run would have been
killed; under the new one it passed. So the raise is doing real work, not just
padding idle headroom.

Read it carefully, though. The raise stopped the kills by giving a slow suite
room, not by making it faster, and a 49m42s run leaves only about 10 minutes
before the 60-minute wall. The flake is gone; the slowness that caused it is
not. If `bats-app` keeps creeping, the next conversation is about splitting the
suite, not raising the cap again.

Separately, the flake that dominated last quarter has gone quiet on its own.
`app-readiness-timeout` logged 25 in W24, then 2 in W26 and 1 in W28, and none
this week. No timeout change explains it: the collapse lands between W23 and
W24, while the caps moved on 2026-05-21 and 2026-07-14. The cause is still
unknown, and the all-time ranking now points at a target that stopped moving, so
anyone picking work off that ranking should start elsewhere.

Two `main` failures resisted a matcher, and I recorded them instead: a Windows
step that died with exit 3840 while its suite passed 123 of 123, and a `read
ETIMEDOUT` in `check-update-versions` that a re-run cleared three hours later.
Each happened once, and each leaves a signature broad enough to swallow a real
failure, so a pattern would hide more than it catches. A second occurrence of
either changes that.

<summary>How this report is built</summary>

`ci-sweep weekly --week 2026-W29` over `sweep.db`, one row per **failed job** — a run can fail on one matrix leg out of many, so the job is the unit.

- **Known flake** — a job whose log matched a `matchers.yaml` pattern. Pattern matching only, no LLM.
- **One flake per job** — when several matchers fire, the job counts under the one whose surface appears **earliest** in the log, because the first failure is the root and what follows is fallout: a suite killed at its wall-clock cap logs a failed wait on the way out. The others are recorded but not counted, and only a true tie is left uncounted.
- **Proven flake** — the identical job passed on a re-run of the same commit, so the code cannot be at fault.
- **Unrecognized** — no matcher fired. On `main` it earns analysis; on a PR branch it earns only a count, because PR failures are dev breakage, not CI health.
- **No log** — the log expired or was never uploaded, so the sweep could not classify the failure. A data gap, not a clean bill.
- **What failed** — for a known flake, the matcher's summary; otherwise the failing test and command, or the job error for a failure that never reached its tests. `--fetch` names the rest, at one download each.
- Counts cover only jobs the sweep recorded. A period never swept reads as zero failures.

</details>
